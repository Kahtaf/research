`tests/generated.c` is generated by `scripts/generate_tests.py`: exact final helper contents (only Linux include lines removed), final callback/pointer/stop bodies, original callback bodies, original common conversion bodies, actual ALSA transfer wrappers, original DL1/UL1 IRQ handlers, and actual ARM64-path ALSA I/O-helper bodies. `test-production-bodies.json` records extraction hashes. These substitutions cannot validate real ARM DMA coherency, IRQ latency, scheduling, MMIO bus behavior or physical routing.

The suite exhausts every frame-aligned offset and transfer length through a256-byte ring for mono/stereo16-bit and32-bit containers in both directions, including wrap and64-byte boundaries. It tests invalid and insufficient rings, malformed/overflowing/zero requests, null control, invalid channel/position, user faults before/after64 and256-byte boundaries, partial user-buffer mutation, allocation failure, exact IRQ progress and counters, stop generation, suspend, DMA expiry, post-xrun IRQ accounting, and two real concurrent copy callers. Original false successes remain assertions in the passing test, not logs from a rewritten approximation. The exact ALSA transfer wrappers prove negative status propagation. Full ALSA read/write-loop application-pointer behavior is traced from source, not simulated as a new acceptance algorithm.

`python3 scripts/verify_bundle.py` verifies source/compiler/object hash agreement, unchanged before/after fixture pins, and applies the patch to disposable copies to compare all three resulting files. The generated after-manifest is a proposed future overlay, not a claim that a physical fixture has been patched.
