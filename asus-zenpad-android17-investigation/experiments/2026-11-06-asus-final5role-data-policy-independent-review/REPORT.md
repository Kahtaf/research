**Proof boundaries, not new findings.** The auditor is a relational comparator and does not hash-authenticate its arguments. As a deliberate boundary test, supplying a candidate with an extra helper FD edge and a correspondingly altered frozen reference makes it pass. The same candidate with the genuine pinned reference fails. The existing INTEGRATION instructions already require the exact base/reference hashes and assertions enabled; those requirements must remain binding. The smallest safeguard at any future caller boundary is to check lengths and hashes before invocation, rather than treating arbitrary matching references as trusted. This is not a bypass under the reviewed pinned-input contract.

“Only BLKGETSIZE64” here means the singleton low-16-bit SELinux ioctl key `0x1272` for the inspected daemon/helper pairs. Xperm tables do not distinguish upper command-encoding bits or independently enforce path/FD identity, kernel ioctl handling or other runtime authorization. A fresh stock init → generic `block_device` query has coarse ioctl permission, zero matching xperm rules and 65,536 permitted keys, including `0x1277`. Therefore the report's explicitly pair-scoped claim is justified; a global all-actor or all-command claim would be overbroad. See [ioctl-scope-boundary.json](evidence/ioctl-scope-boundary.json). No such global claim is accepted here.

Raw byte equality proves the actual artifact's unchanged serialized fields. It does not replace fresh effective evaluation of the changed edge; the sampled effective matrix does not by itself prove every policy decision. The independent equality proof supplies the non-delta coverage. None of these facts authenticate future helper/container bytes, inherited descriptors, graph reachability or application of the policy on a kernel.
