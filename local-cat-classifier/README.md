# A local cat identity classifier for a fixed camera

**Research packet for a future [engineering post](https://github.com/Kahtaf/personal-blog).** It contains the experimental record, aggregate evidence, and instructions for repeating the method with your own camera crops.

**Status on November 20, 2026.** A 1.91 MB MobileNetV3 Small identified Eli, Pixel, or Empty on this particular setup. It beat the saved Gemini answers in a retrospective development replay. Fresh paired results favored it by one bundle from one Eli visit, with zero Pixel visits, so a prospective win across both cats remains unverified. The retrained model's 48-hour HA shadow trial has started. Its outcome is pending.

## Data and labels

1. **Start with the existing tracker evidence.** Export the ledger and saved three-frame crops for Recent and Archived candidates. Include rejected motion, both cameras, and difficult lighting. Preserve the original bytes, acquisition intervals, tracker event IDs, model provenance, and review overrides privately. The original seed contained 487 reviewed labeled frames across day-disjoint training, validation and evaluation partitions. The grouped ledger export later joined 3,580 complete bundles and yielded 10,593 unique decoded images. Many images stayed outside supervised training.
2. **Treat the task as identity and presence in the crop.** Review the frame rather than trusting the tracker visit verdict. A cat walking past a tray can be labeled Eli or Pixel even when the visit verdict is false. Mark blur, ambiguity and two-cat cases for review or exclusion. Keep proposal labels and human-confirmed labels as separate fields. The grouped full fit had 5,083 labels, including 2,905 weak Empty labels based on close background similarity. Do not count these as independently annotated controls when evaluating.
3. **Pilot Gemini annotation before scaling.** Give one verified reference per cat, then one target crop. The 100-crop pilot spread sampling across events and held out 24 later crops for its initial training comparison. Gemini proposed 6 Eli, 1 Pixel and 93 Neither. The user corrected one Eli to Pixel. The final counts were 5 Eli, 2 Pixel and 93 Neither. The subsequent full-data fit included all 100, so the old pilot holdout cannot evaluate that fit independently.
4. **Audit the unreviewed folder by content.** Some files physically remained under Needs label after labeling or training. Comparing decoded pixels and source intervals identified 860 distinct unused crops from 307 tracker events outside known evaluation windows. Gemini proposed Neither for every one, including seven crops earlier local models had ranked as likely Pixel. The user verified all 860 as Neither. The 860-image run's estimated paid-tier cost was $0.73 USD from reported tokens. The pilot was about $0.085 USD. [Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing/)
5. **Split before fitting.** Hold out whole dates or groups of visits. Exclude shared event IDs and exact matches in decoded pixels. Apply a five-minute embargo around the split to drop adjacent near-duplicate evidence. For independent benchmarks, preserve a new time window after freezing the checkpoint and all thresholds. The 134-bundle replay used date-held-out refits, while its aggregation rule was chosen with those results in view. Its accuracy is development evidence.

## Models and tradeoffs

- **Frozen visual features.** Early MobileNetV3 Large and DINOv3 ViT-S/16 heads both got 54/54 on the first later-day test, which contained only one Pixel group. The two primary ONNX files were 11.34 and 82.55 MiB, with median per-frame Mac timings of 11.42 and 44.77 ms under that test's two-thread setup. A separate native Frigate experiment tested MobileNetV2 0.35 INT8 at 789,336 bytes and scored 52/54 on the same later-day set, including a confident false Empty. The early DINO, Large and native Frigate benchmarks use different runtime contracts from the final Small and should not be combined into a single speed ranking. [Earlier experiment provenance](notes.md#earlier-spikes)
- **Aggregation changed the result.** The 134-bundle comparison gave Large 88/134, original Small 85/134 and Gemini 122/134. The original aggregation required agreement in at least two confident frames and vetoed a named cat when another confident frame was Empty. A cat can appear in only one sampled frame. Allowing one confident name, with abstention on confident conflicting names, raised Small to 133/134 on that development replay. [Original comparison](evidence/development-original-models.json) · [Aggregation replay](evidence/development-aggregation.json)
- **Smaller paths that failed.** Simple fixed-background subtraction bypassed zero of 174 fresh bundles and increased median runtime. A 160px primary classifier was faster but made a false Pixel call on a cleaner in development. The first static INT8 attempt was about 1.25 MB and lost decision parity on all 174 fresh bundles. These failures apply to the tested implementations and configurations. [Background shortcut](evidence/foreground-shortcut.json) · [160px classifier](evidence/low-resolution.json) · [INT8 run](evidence/int8-attempt.json)
- **A small CPU package.** The final graph stores larger weights in FP16 and casts them to float32 when used. It shares one frozen backbone across the 160px screen, 320px classifier, and two extra fallback logits. The 160px screen returns Empty only if all three frames score at least 0.99 for Empty. The 320px classifier uses the one-confident-name rule at 0.8. Separate presence and identity heads run on the same features and are consulted on abstention. That fallback recovered two missed Pixel bundles from one group in retrospective evaluation. There were no paired Gemini answers for those two.
- **Runtime cost depends on the mix.** Warming one CPU thread on an M4 Mac, with decoding included, the combined graph reduced the median of the mixed 32-bundle set from 27.7 to 16.1 ms. The visible-cat path went from 28.0 to 33.0 ms. The benchmark had four Eli-visible bundles from a single visit and 28 Empty bundles. Local ARM64 Linux verification matched all 182 checked bundles and used under 94 MiB peak process RSS in the replay. The ONNX file size is 1,908,020 bytes. [Combined benchmark](evidence/combined-runtime.json) · [Linux verification](evidence/linux-runtime.json) · [Held-out screen](evidence/development-screen-and-fallback.json)
- **More Empty helped decisiveness.** Adding the 860 confirmed Empty frames increased the 320px fit from 5,083 to 5,943 labels. Explicit Empty rose from 40/42 to 42/42 in development. Eli stayed 51/51 and Pixel 40/41. The mixed no-cat-name metric stayed 133/134. The 160px screen and frozen feature extractor were reused. [Retraining comparison](evidence/empty-retraining.json)

## Shadow mode in Home Assistant

The app observes the tracker's saved crops and Gemini decisions through read paths. It writes only to its own trial state and private evidence store, never to the production ledger. Each trial persists its experiment ID, start, deadline, policy and model hashes before collecting candidates. It hashes each three-frame bundle, retains baseline history for delayed Gemini answers, and preserves abstention, missing evidence and blank images. Use a bounded hourly catch-up in addition to head polling and recheck cohort completeness after a restart.

The initial Large run processed 1,228 of 1,265 candidates and stopped at the fixed deadline. It had 16 provisional groups among high-confidence named Gemini visits and only 60% accepted agreement coverage. One saved Eli visit override coincided with local Empty. Binary corruption in the ingress proxy prevented independent image review of that run, so the actual false-Empty rate is unknown. A binary-safe evidence path is part of the reproduction workflow. [Original assessment aggregates](evidence/first-72h-shadow.json)

The 48-hour follow-up uses the 860-Empty retrain, a separate experiment ID, the 160/320/fallback policy, read-only media, a single inference thread, and an independent shutdown/RSS guard. Gemini and the tracker retain production authority. Frame labels alone cannot establish sustained occupancy, a litter-box visit, or elimination. A separate visit policy and independently reviewed fresh Pixel and Eli visits are required before a switch.
