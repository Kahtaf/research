# When You Get Here — VERSION B
### "The Parents' Hope" · ages 2–5 (and for the grown-ups reading aloud)
*A standalone book that braids anticipation with the worry and anxiousness of a waiting NICU family, released at homecoming. Squarely for NICU parents.*

**POV:** A parent (voice works for either or both), speaking to the baby who came too soon.
**Register:** Tender, honest, aching, then flooded with relief. Hope and fear in the same breath.
**Word count:** ~320
**Refrain / title phrase:** *"When you get here"* — begins as a hope, deepens into a prayer on the hard night, and is finally answered ("You're here. You're home.").
**NICU handling:** clearly a baby who arrived early and is cared for elsewhere while the family waits — but no equipment tour, no machines on the page. The fear is named plainly and gently, the way the best comfort books do, then resolved.

---

**SPREAD 1** — *Two cupped hands, impossibly small baby; warm light.*
> You came before we were ready.
> So small, we could hold you in two hands.
> Now you rest somewhere warm and bright,
> where gentle people help you grow.
> And we are learning how to wait.

**SPREAD 2** — *A parent at a dark window, the words almost visible.*
> When you get here.
> I say it in the morning.
> I say it in the dark.
> I say it like a wish,
> like a prayer,
> like a promise to us both.

**SPREAD 3** — *The waiting room at home: a nursery, a light left on.*
> When you get here,
> there's a room with your name on the wall,
> and a blanket your brother picked out,
> and a light we leave on all night long,
> so you will know the way.

**SPREAD 4** — *Big brother whispering to an empty crib.*
> When you get here,
> your brother has a hundred things to show you.
> He is practicing being big.
> He whispers to your empty crib,
> "Hurry up. I'm ready."

**SPREAD 5** — *Close: a finger beside a tiny hand; a parent's face near.*
> Right now you are so small.
> Smaller than the teddy bear.
> Smaller than we ever dreamed a person could be.
> But your heartbeat is loud.
> We listen to it like a song.

**SPREAD 6** — *The nightly drive home; empty car seat.*
> Every night, we have to leave you there
> and drive home without you.
> The car is very quiet.
> The house is very quiet.
> When you get here, we whisper. When you get here.

**SPREAD 7** — *The hard night. A parent holding on. Muted, close.*
> Some nights are long.
> Some nights we are so afraid
> we can only hold onto those four small words.
> When you get here.
> When you get here.
> Please. When you get here.

**SPREAD 8** — *Turn: warmth returns. The baby a little bigger, a little stronger.*
> But you are stronger than your size.
> Ounce by ounce. Day by day.
> You breathe. You grow. You hold on.
> And slowly, so slowly,
> the fear grows smaller
> and the hope grows big.

**SPREAD 9** — *A nurse (soft, warm) and the words.*
> Until one morning
> they say the words
> we have been holding our breath to hear:
> "She's ready.
> She's strong enough.
> She can go home."

**SPREAD 10** — *Wrapping her up; the whole family in one small car.*
> We wrap you up. We buckle you in.
> The car is not quiet this time.
> Your brother cannot stop smiling.
> We drive home
> with everyone we love in one small car.

**SPREAD 11** — *Home at last; the worry drifting off like smoke.*
> And then you're here.
> Really here. In our arms. In your room. In the light.
> All the waiting,
> all the worry,
> folds up small and quiet
> and drifts away.

**SPREAD 12 — FINAL** — *The family together; the night light glowing.*
> You're here.
> You're finally, finally here.
> We used to whisper when you get here
> like a wish we weren't sure would come true.
> Now we get to say the other words—
> the ones we love the most:
> You're home.
> Welcome. We waited for you.

---
*Craft: the refrain changes weight — wish (S2) → whisper in the quiet (S6) → prayer at the peak of fear (S7) → answered (S12). Meter tightens on the hard night and loosens into relief. Names the fear plainly ("so afraid"), then resolves it, the way Owl Babies and the lullaby tradition earn trust. The parent-to-baby voice lets a 2–3-year-old sibling listen in on how fiercely a new baby is loved — quietly reassuring them they are loved the same way. Ends on the two words the whole book is reaching for: "You're home."*
